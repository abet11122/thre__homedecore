# Photo shot list

Every article currently uses stock photography. Replacing even a few heroes with your own
photographs is the strongest single signal of first-hand experience you can add, and it is
the one thing on this site that cannot be written.

**How to use this:** shoot a post's photos, drop them in `photos-inbox/<slug>/` with a
`.txt` alongside each (line 1 alt text, line 2 optional caption), and run `npm run photos`.
See `photos-inbox/README.md` for the mechanics.

**Name the hero `hero`** — that replaces the stock hero and generates the Pinterest pin in
one go. Everything else can be `photo-1`, `photo-2`, and so on.

## Shooting notes

**Daylight, near a window, no flash.** Phone cameras are fine. A flash flattens texture,
which is the thing most of these articles are about.

**Shoot the "before" first, and badly lit if that is the truth.** The honest before is more
convincing than the tidy after, and it is the shot nobody else on the internet has.

**Same position for before and after.** Mark where you stood. A pair shot from two different
spots is worth far less than a pair shot from one.

**Horizontal for the hero.** The pin is generated as a 2:3 crop from the centre of
attention, so leave some room around the subject.

**Write the alt text while you remember what is in the shot.** It goes on line 1 of the
`.txt` file and it is what a screen reader and a search engine both read.

**Include the mess where the article mentions it.** Several of these posts argue that a
surface has to survive real use. A photograph of an immaculate hall table undercuts the
article; one with the post and keys on it proves the point.

## Why this matters for the site

Every article currently states, in a comment the reader never sees, that its photographs are
stock. The About page says so publicly. Replacing them is what turns this from a well-written
archive into a first-hand one, and the five priority-1 posts would do most of that work.
