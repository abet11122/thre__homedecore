Christmas crafts for preschoolers work best when the project is short, the materials are simple, and the result is something the child can recognise as their own work. At this age, the process matters more than the product — but the best projects produce both a good experience and a result worth keeping.

The ideas below are all achievable for children aged 2–5 with minimal adult help.

## 1. Handprint Christmas Tree Card

Press the child's hand into green paint and stamp it onto card with fingers spread to create a tree shape. Add a star at the top with a finger dip in yellow paint and a trunk with brown paint.

Write the child's name and age on the back. This card will be kept for years.

## 2. Fingerprint Baubles Card

Draw a simple Christmas tree outline on card and have the child press their fingertip into different colours of paint to create fingerprint baubles on the tree.

This is achievable for children as young as 18 months with adult guidance.

## 3. Paper Plate Wreath

Cut the centre from a paper plate to create a ring. Have the child paint it green, then stick on red dot stickers or fingerprint dots for berries.

Add a ribbon bow at the top when dry. This takes about 20 minutes and produces a decoration the child can hang up.

## 4. Cotton Wool Snowman

Glue cotton wool balls onto black card to create a snowman shape. Add a hat cut from black paper, a scarf cut from fabric scraps, and button eyes made from small stickers.

This is a low-mess craft that produces a charming result.

## 5. Sticker Christmas Tree

Draw a large triangle on paper and give the child a sheet of round stickers in different colours to decorate it as a Christmas tree.

This is suitable for children as young as 2 and requires no paint or glue.

## 6. Toilet Roll Reindeer

Cover a toilet roll tube with brown paint. Add googly eyes, a red pom-pom nose, and pipe cleaner antlers. Leave to dry.

This takes about 20 minutes including drying time and produces a decoration children are very proud of.

## 7. Scrunched Tissue Paper Wreath

Cut a ring from card and have the child scrunch small squares of green tissue paper and glue them all over the ring. Add red dot stickers for berries when dry.

The scrunching action is satisfying for small hands and the result looks genuinely festive.

## 8. Handprint Reindeer Card

Press the child's hand into brown paint and stamp it onto card with the thumb pointing up. The thumb becomes the reindeer's head, the fingers become the antlers. Add googly eyes and a red dot nose.

This is one of the most popular preschool Christmas crafts and produces a card grandparents treasure.

## 9. Decorated Biscuit

Bake plain round biscuits and let the child decorate them with white icing and sprinkles. This is technically a food activity but the decorated biscuits can be given as gifts or hung on the tree with a ribbon.

## 10. Paper Chain Garland

Pre-cut strips of red, green, and white paper and let the child link them into a chain using a glue stick. This is achievable for children aged 3 and above and produces a decoration they can hang in their room.

## 11. Painted Pine Cone

Give the child a pine cone and let them paint it with gold, silver, or red paint. Add glitter while the paint is wet.

When dry, tie a ribbon around the top for hanging. This is a simple craft that produces a beautiful tree decoration.

## 12. Fingerprint Snowman

Have the child press three fingerprints in a vertical line onto card using white paint to create a snowman. Add a hat, scarf, and buttons with a fine pen when dry.

This is achievable for children as young as 2 with adult help.

## Final Thoughts

Preschool Christmas crafts are most successful when the project is short, the materials are ready before the child sits down, and the adult's expectations are focused on enjoyment rather than perfection. A slightly wonky handprint tree made by a three-year-old is one of the most beautiful Christmas decorations in existence.
