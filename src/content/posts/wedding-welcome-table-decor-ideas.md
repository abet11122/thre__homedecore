The wedding welcome table is the first thing guests encounter when they arrive at the reception. It sets the tone for the whole event and needs to be both beautiful and functional — guests need to find their seats quickly, so the seating information must be clear and easy to navigate.

The ideas below cover every element of a wedding welcome table, from the sign to the favours.

## 1. The Welcome Sign

A welcome sign — "Welcome to Our Wedding", the couple's names and date, or a simple "Mr & Mrs [Name]" — is the visual anchor of the welcome table.

Use a large mirror, a wooden board, an acrylic panel, or a framed print. The sign should be visible from the entrance so guests know immediately where to go.

## 2. The Seating Chart

The seating chart is the most important functional element of the welcome table. Display it clearly — on a large board, a mirror, or a series of framed cards — so guests can find their table quickly.

Organise the seating chart alphabetically by guest name rather than by table number for the easiest navigation.

## 3. Escort Cards

Individual escort cards — one per guest or per couple — displayed in a creative way are an alternative to a large seating chart.

Display them in a frame, on a string of twine with mini pegs, in a box of sand or rice, or tucked into a floral arrangement.

## 4. The Floral Arrangement

A generous floral arrangement on one side of the welcome table adds beauty and signals that the table is part of the reception decor rather than a functional afterthought.

Use flowers that match the reception centerpieces for a cohesive look.

## 5. The Guest Book

A guest book on the welcome table gives guests something to do while they wait and creates a keepsake for the couple.

Alternatives to a traditional guest book include a Polaroid photo station, a puzzle guests can sign, or a framed print guests can write messages on.

## 6. Wedding Favours

Displaying wedding favours on the welcome table — rather than at each place setting — allows guests to choose their own and creates a generous, abundant display.

Label the favours clearly and add a small sign explaining what they are.

## 7. A Personal Photo Display

A small display of photos of the couple — childhood photos, engagement photos, or travel photos — adds a personal touch to the welcome table.

Use matching frames or a simple string of photos on twine.

## 8. The Menu Card

A menu card displayed on the welcome table lets guests know what to expect before they sit down. This is particularly useful for guests with dietary requirements.

## 9. The Table Runner

A table runner that matches the reception decor ties the welcome table into the overall aesthetic. Use the same runner material — burlap, linen, or a floral print — as the reception tables.

## 10. Candles and Greenery

Candles and greenery fill any gaps in the welcome table display and add warmth. Use the same candle style and greenery as the reception tables for a cohesive look.

## Final Thoughts

A wedding welcome table is most effective when it is beautiful, functional, and easy to navigate. Start with the seating chart as the functional centrepiece, add a welcome sign and a floral arrangement, and fill in with personal touches. The table should feel like a genuine welcome — warm, personal, and clearly organised.
