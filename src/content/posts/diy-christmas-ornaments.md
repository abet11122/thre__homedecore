DIY Christmas ornaments are one of the most rewarding holiday crafts. They are personal, they look genuinely handmade in the best sense, and they become part of the story of your Christmas tree over the years.

The ideas below range from five-minute projects to slightly more involved ones, and all of them produce results worth keeping.

## 1. Glitter-Filled Clear Ball Ornaments

<!-- IDEA 1: [MY MEASUREMENTS / COST / TIME: record constraints for this option]. [WHAT I TRIED AND WHAT WENT WRONG: actual result only]. Add suitability, tradeoff, installation and maintenance evidence. -->

Remove the cap from a clear glass or plastic ball ornament, pour in a small amount of fine glitter, replace the cap, and shake gently. The glitter settles into a beautiful, sparkling interior.

Use a single colour of glitter for the most elegant result, or layer two complementary colours.

## 2. Painted Dipped Ornaments

<!-- IDEA 2: [MY MEASUREMENTS / COST / TIME: record constraints for this option]. [WHAT I TRIED AND WHAT WENT WRONG: actual result only]. Add suitability, tradeoff, installation and maintenance evidence. -->

Dip the bottom half of a plain white or clear ornament into a small pot of acrylic paint. Hold it over the pot for a moment to let the excess drip off, then hang it to dry.

This produces a clean, modern look that works in any colour palette.

## 3. Dried Botanical Ornaments

<!-- IDEA 3: [MY MEASUREMENTS / COST / TIME: record constraints for this option]. [WHAT I TRIED AND WHAT WENT WRONG: actual result only]. Add suitability, tradeoff, installation and maintenance evidence. -->

Fill a clear ball ornament with dried botanicals — lavender, rose petals, eucalyptus, or small pine cones. The botanical material is visible through the glass and creates a beautiful, natural look.

Dry fresh botanicals in the oven at a very low temperature before using them.

## 4. Personalised Name Ornaments

<!-- IDEA 4: [MY MEASUREMENTS / COST / TIME: record constraints for this option]. [WHAT I TRIED AND WHAT WENT WRONG: actual result only]. Add suitability, tradeoff, installation and maintenance evidence. -->

Write a name, date, or short message on a plain ornament using a paint pen or fine permanent marker. Add the year to create a keepsake that records each Christmas.

These make excellent gifts and are always more appreciated than a bought ornament.

## 5. Salt Dough Ornaments

<!-- IDEA 5: [MY MEASUREMENTS / COST / TIME: record constraints for this option]. [WHAT I TRIED AND WHAT WENT WRONG: actual result only]. Add suitability, tradeoff, installation and maintenance evidence. -->

Mix one cup of plain flour, half a cup of salt, and half a cup of water to make salt dough. Roll it out, cut shapes with cookie cutters, make a hole at the top, and bake at 120°C for two to three hours.

Paint and seal with Mod Podge when cool. Salt dough ornaments last for years if kept dry.

## 6. Fabric Scrap Ornaments

<!-- IDEA 6: [MY MEASUREMENTS / COST / TIME: record constraints for this option]. [WHAT I TRIED AND WHAT WENT WRONG: actual result only]. Add suitability, tradeoff, installation and maintenance evidence. -->

Cut small squares of fabric in seasonal colours and patterns. Stuff a simple fabric shape — a star, a heart, or a simple ball — with a small amount of stuffing and sew or glue it closed.

These are particularly good for using up fabric scraps from other projects.

## 7. Cinnamon Applesauce Ornaments

<!-- IDEA 7: [MY MEASUREMENTS / COST / TIME: record constraints for this option]. [WHAT I TRIED AND WHAT WENT WRONG: actual result only]. Add suitability, tradeoff, installation and maintenance evidence. -->

Mix one cup of ground cinnamon with three quarters of a cup of applesauce to make a fragrant dough. Roll it out, cut shapes, make a hole at the top, and leave to dry for 24–48 hours.

These ornaments smell wonderful and last for years. They do not need painting — the cinnamon colour is beautiful on its own.

## 8. Mod Podge Tissue Paper Ornaments

<!-- IDEA 8: [MY MEASUREMENTS / COST / TIME: record constraints for this option]. [WHAT I TRIED AND WHAT WENT WRONG: actual result only]. Add suitability, tradeoff, installation and maintenance evidence. -->

Tear small pieces of tissue paper in seasonal colours and apply them to a plain ornament using Mod Podge. Overlap the pieces and smooth them down as you go.

When dry, apply a final coat of Mod Podge to seal. The result is a colourful, slightly translucent ornament.

## 9. Twine-Wrapped Ornaments

<!-- IDEA 9: [MY MEASUREMENTS / COST / TIME: record constraints for this option]. [WHAT I TRIED AND WHAT WENT WRONG: actual result only]. Add suitability, tradeoff, installation and maintenance evidence. -->

Wrap a plain ornament tightly with natural twine, securing the end with a small dot of glue. The result is a simple, textural ornament that suits a farmhouse or natural Christmas aesthetic.

Add a small sprig of dried greenery or a tiny ornament to the top for extra detail.

## 10. Photo Ornaments

<!-- IDEA 10: [MY MEASUREMENTS / COST / TIME: record constraints for this option]. [WHAT I TRIED AND WHAT WENT WRONG: actual result only]. Add suitability, tradeoff, installation and maintenance evidence. -->

Print a small photo — a family portrait, a child's face, or a favourite memory — and attach it to a plain ornament using Mod Podge. Seal with a final coat.

Photo ornaments become the most treasured decorations on the tree over time.

## Final Thoughts

<!-- IDEA 11: [MY MEASUREMENTS / COST / TIME: record constraints for this option]. [WHAT I TRIED AND WHAT WENT WRONG: actual result only]. Add suitability, tradeoff, installation and maintenance evidence. -->

DIY Christmas ornaments are worth making because they become part of the story of your Christmas tree. A tree decorated with handmade ornaments, each with a memory attached, is more meaningful than one decorated with shop-bought sets. Start with one or two techniques and make a few extras — they make excellent gifts.
