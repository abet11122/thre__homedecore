Friendsgiving is one of the warmest celebrations of the year — a gathering built on gratitude, good food, and the company of chosen family. The decor should reflect that warmth: personal, seasonal, and genuinely welcoming rather than formal or elaborate.

The ideas below cover the table, the room, and the personal touches that make a Friendsgiving feel special.

## 1. Set a Long Table With a Garland Runner

A long table with a garland runner of dried leaves, eucalyptus, and wheat, with candles and small gourds placed among it, is the most impactful Friendsgiving setup.

Use a mix of taper candles and pillar candles at varying heights for the warmest light.

## 2. Use Mismatched Crockery Intentionally

Friendsgiving is the perfect occasion for mismatched crockery. Mix plates, bowls, and glasses in complementary colours and textures for a relaxed, collected look.

Use a consistent napkin colour to tie the mismatched elements together.

## 3. Add Handwritten Name Cards

A handwritten name card at each place setting makes guests feel individually welcomed. Write names on kraft card, luggage tags, or small chalkboard labels.

Add a dried leaf or a sprig of rosemary under each card for a seasonal finishing touch.

## 4. Create a Gratitude Board

A chalkboard or a large piece of kraft paper on the wall where guests can write what they are grateful for creates a personal, meaningful focal point for the gathering.

Provide chalk pens or markers and encourage guests to add to it throughout the evening.

## 5. Display Photos of the Group

A string of photos of the friend group — from previous Friendsgivings, holidays, and everyday moments — hung on a length of twine creates a personal display that celebrates the relationships being honoured.

Use mini pegs or paper clips to attach the photos.

## 6. Use Candles Generously

Candles create the warmest possible atmosphere for a Friendsgiving gathering. Use them on the table, on the mantelpiece, and on any other surfaces in the room.

Group candles in odd numbers and vary the heights for the most interesting effect.

## 7. Add a Welcome Sign

A chalkboard sign or a wooden sign with "Friendsgiving" or "Gather" at the entrance sets the tone before guests arrive.

## 8. Style a Drinks Station

A styled drinks station — a sideboard or console table with glasses, a pitcher of cider or wine, and a few seasonal decorations — creates a welcoming focal point and keeps the table clear for food.

Add a small sign with the drink options and a few candles for warmth.

## 9. Use Cloth Napkins in Fall Colours

Cloth napkins in rust, ochre, deep green, or warm cream make an immediate difference to the feel of the table. Fold them simply and tie with a length of twine or a sprig of rosemary.

## 10. Add a Pumpkin Pie Display

A small display of pumpkin pies or other desserts on a sideboard or kitchen counter, styled with a few candles and some dried leaves, creates a warm, abundant focal point for the food.

## 11. Keep the Atmosphere Relaxed

Friendsgiving decor should feel warm and personal rather than formal or elaborate. Avoid anything that requires guests to be careful — fragile decorations, elaborate table settings, or anything that makes the gathering feel like a performance rather than a celebration.

## Final Thoughts

The best Friendsgiving decor is the kind that makes guests feel immediately at home. A warm table, personal touches at each place, candles, and a gratitude board are all you need. The gathering itself — the food, the conversation, and the company — is the decoration.
