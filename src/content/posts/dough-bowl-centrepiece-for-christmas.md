A dough bowl centrepiece is one of the most popular Christmas decorating ideas for good reason. The organic shape of the bowl adds warmth and texture that a flat tray or a symmetrical vase cannot match, and the arrangement can be as simple or as layered as you like.

This guide covers everything from choosing the right bowl to the exact order in which to add each element.

## Choosing the Right Bowl

A wooden dough bowl between 40cm and 60cm long is the most versatile size for a dining table centrepiece. A smaller bowl — 25–35cm — works well on a coffee table, sideboard, or mantelpiece.

The bowl does not need to be antique or expensive. New dough bowls in a range of sizes are widely available, and the arrangement inside is what creates the visual impact.

## The Base Layer: Greenery

Start by filling the bowl with fresh or faux greenery. This is the most important step — a generous greenery base is what makes the arrangement look full and natural.

Use a mix of textures if possible: pine or cedar for structure, eucalyptus for softer, trailing stems, and a few sprigs of holly or berries for colour. Let the greenery spill slightly over the edges of the bowl.

## Adding Candles

Add candles next, before any ornaments or accessories. Use an odd number — three or five — and vary the heights.

A combination of taper candles in a holder, a pillar candle, and a small votive creates the most interesting silhouette. Place the tallest candle slightly off-centre rather than in the middle.

Use flameless LED candles if the arrangement will be left unattended or if the greenery is very close to the flame.

## Adding Ornaments

Add ornaments in one or two colours, tucking them among the greenery at different depths. Use an odd number and vary the sizes.

Matte ornaments often look better in a dough bowl than shiny ones — they absorb light rather than reflecting it, which suits the organic, natural aesthetic of the arrangement.

## Adding Natural Accents

Pine cones, dried orange slices, cinnamon sticks, and seed heads add texture and fragrance. Tuck them among the greenery and ornaments, filling any gaps.

These elements are inexpensive and make the arrangement smell as good as it looks.

## Adding Ribbon or Fabric

A length of velvet or wired ribbon woven through the arrangement adds colour and movement. Use a ribbon that picks up one of the colours in the ornaments.

Wired ribbon holds its shape better than unwired ribbon and is easier to arrange.

## Finishing Touches

Step back and look at the arrangement from the angle it will be viewed from most often. Fill any obvious gaps with small sprigs of greenery or a pine cone.

Check that the candles are stable and that no greenery is touching the flame.

## Placement Options

A dough bowl centrepiece works in several locations:

- **Dining table** — place it in the centre and make sure it is low enough to see across
- **Coffee table** — use a smaller bowl and keep the arrangement lower
- **Mantelpiece** — a long, low arrangement suits a mantelpiece well
- **Sideboard or console table** — a larger, more generous arrangement works here because it does not need to be seen across

## Caring for the Arrangement

If using fresh greenery, mist it lightly every two to three days and keep the bowl away from radiators and direct heat. Fresh greenery in a cool room lasts one to two weeks.

For a longer-lasting arrangement, use high-quality faux greenery. The best faux pine and cedar is indistinguishable from fresh at normal viewing distance.

## Final Thoughts

A dough bowl centrepiece is one of the most rewarding Christmas decorating projects because the result looks genuinely considered and the process is straightforward. Start with a generous greenery base, add candles at varying heights, then fill in with ornaments and natural accents. The bowl does the structural work — your job is to fill it well.
