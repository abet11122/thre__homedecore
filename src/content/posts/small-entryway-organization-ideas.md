An entryway needs to hold the things used on the way in and out, while leaving a safe route through the home. Start with the door and the people using it, then choose storage that fits the available space. Any dimensions below are examples for planning, not a substitute for measuring your own entry.

Once you have measured a recess or shelf, the [storage fit checker](/tools/storage-fit-checker/) can compare it with a product's outside dimensions.

## Map the movement first

<!-- IDEA 1: [MY MEASUREMENTS / COST / TIME: record constraints for this option]. [WHAT I TRIED AND WHAT WENT WRONG: actual result only]. Add suitability, tradeoff, installation and maintenance evidence. -->

Open the front door fully and mark where it swings. Do the same for a nearby closet, bathroom, or stair door. Measure the clear walking width at the narrowest point, then measure it again with a coat hanging on the proposed hook. A cabinet's listed depth is not its full impact: include handles, shoes protruding from open shelves, and the space needed to open its doors.

Use painter's tape to mark the outline of a proposed cabinet or bench on the floor. Walk through with a bag and, if relevant, a stroller or mobility aid. If access feels restricted, choose a shallower solution or move storage elsewhere. Check local accessibility or building requirements where they apply.

## Count what belongs at the door

<!-- IDEA 2: [MY MEASUREMENTS / COST / TIME: record constraints for this option]. [WHAT I TRIED AND WHAT WENT WRONG: actual result only]. Add suitability, tradeoff, installation and maintenance evidence. -->

List daily shoes, coats, bags, keys, and mail separately from occasional items. Give each regular user a reachable hook or cubby if space allows. Store off-season coats and spare footwear outside the entry. A family of four does not need four large shoe racks at the door if each person uses only one or two pairs most days.

## Choose a drop zone by available wall space

<!-- IDEA 3: [MY MEASUREMENTS / COST / TIME: record constraints for this option]. [WHAT I TRIED AND WHAT WENT WRONG: actual result only]. Add suitability, tradeoff, installation and maintenance evidence. -->

| Space available | First option | Trade-off to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Almost no floor area | A small key hook and wall tray | Check permitted fixing and projection from the wall. |
| Narrow wall | Shallow shoe cabinet or slim shelf | Open the cabinet door and recheck the walking route. |
| Wider recess | Bench with accessible shoe storage | Check sitting space, lid clearance, and how shoes dry. |

A hook rail is often more useful than a decorative console when bags and coats are the real source of clutter. A tray helps with small items, but it should have one defined purpose: keys and outgoing mail, for example, rather than everything brought home.

## Example layout calculation

<!-- IDEA 4: [MY MEASUREMENTS / COST / TIME: record constraints for this option]. [WHAT I TRIED AND WHAT WENT WRONG: actual result only]. Add suitability, tradeoff, installation and maintenance evidence. -->

Imagine a wall section that measures 90 cm wide. If a door frame and switch require 15 cm of clearance at one end and a coat must not cover the other 10 cm, the usable wall width is 65 cm. That is an *illustrative calculation*: measure the real obstructions and the actual width of the organizer, including its fixings. Repeat the check at floor level for shoe storage, because the baseboard and door swing may differ from the wall above.

## Keep it easy to reset

<!-- IDEA 5: [MY MEASUREMENTS / COST / TIME: record constraints for this option]. [WHAT I TRIED AND WHAT WENT WRONG: actual result only]. Add suitability, tradeoff, installation and maintenance evidence. -->

Place a small container for mail that needs action near the door, and clear it on a set day. Return shoes to their designated spot when coming in. Reassess after a week: if items still land on the floor, the storage may be too full, too far away, or too difficult to open. Change that friction before buying more furniture.

If the entry shares space with the living room, the [apartment organization plan](/post/small-apartment-organization-ideas/) helps assign the remaining storage by zone.
