A tiered tray is one of the most popular seasonal decorating formats because it creates a layered, abundant display in a contained footprint. The key is using the right number of objects per tier and varying the heights within each level.

The ideas below cover everything from the basic structure to specific fall styling ideas.

## The Basic Structure

A well-styled tiered tray follows a simple hierarchy:

- **Top tier:** the smallest objects — a mini pumpkin, a small candle, a pine cone
- **Middle tier:** medium objects and the anchor sign — a chalkboard, a small lantern, a gourd
- **Bottom tier:** the largest objects — a larger pumpkin, a bundle of wheat, a small dough bowl

Use three to four objects per tier and leave some of each tier's surface visible.

## Fall Colour Palettes for Tiered Trays

- **Classic fall:** rust, ochre, cream, and deep green
- **Neutral fall:** warm white, natural linen, and brass
- **Farmhouse fall:** plaid, galvanised metal, and natural wood
- **Moody fall:** deep burgundy, chocolate brown, and gold

## Specific Styling Ideas

### The Classic Fall Tray

Bottom tier: a small bundle of wheat, a medium pumpkin, and a pine cone. Middle tier: a small chalkboard sign saying "Hello Autumn", a votive candle, and a mini gourd. Top tier: a tiny pumpkin and a cinnamon stick bundle.

### The Neutral Fall Tray

Bottom tier: a cream pumpkin, a natural linen ribbon bundle, and a dried grass sprig. Middle tier: a small wooden sign, a cream candle, and a white mini pumpkin. Top tier: a cotton stem and a small acorn.

### The Farmhouse Fall Tray

Bottom tier: a galvanised metal bucket with a small plant, a plaid ribbon bundle, and a pine cone. Middle tier: a chalkboard sign, a mason jar with a candle, and a mini gourd. Top tier: a small wooden pumpkin and a cinnamon stick.

### The Moody Fall Tray

Bottom tier: a deep burgundy candle, a dark pumpkin, and dried seed heads. Middle tier: a small sign in dark lettering, a votive in a dark vessel, and a dried hydrangea head. Top tier: a small black pumpkin and a dried rose.

## Tips for Varying Heights Within Each Tier

- Use a small riser — an upturned cup, a small book, or a wooden block — under one object per tier
- Stand a small sign upright rather than laying it flat
- Use a tall, narrow candle rather than a short, wide one on the middle tier

## What to Avoid

- Completely covering every tier — leave some surface visible
- Using too many colours — stick to two or three
- Mixing very different styles — farmhouse and modern elements rarely work together on a small tray
- Objects that are too large for the tier — scale matters

## Final Thoughts

A fall tiered tray is most effective when it follows a clear hierarchy — largest at the bottom, smallest at the top — uses a consistent colour palette, and leaves some surface visible on each tier. Start with the sign on the middle tier, add the largest objects to the bottom tier, and fill in the top tier last. The whole process should take under fifteen minutes.
