Fall flower arrangements are some of the most beautiful of the year. The season offers a palette of warm, deep colours — rust, ochre, burgundy, and gold — and a range of textures from fresh dahlias to dried grasses and seed heads.

The ideas below cover fresh arrangements, dried arrangements, and mixed approaches for every skill level.

## 1. The Classic Dahlia Arrangement

Dahlias are the signature fall flower. A generous bunch of dahlias in rust, deep burgundy, or warm orange in a ceramic or terracotta vase is one of the most beautiful fall arrangements you can make.

Cut the stems at an angle and change the water every two days to extend their life.

## 2. The Sunflower and Wheat Bundle

A bundle of sunflowers with wheat and dried grasses in a tall ceramic vase creates a warm, abundant fall arrangement that suits a kitchen, dining room, or entryway.

Sunflowers last one to two weeks in fresh water.

## 3. The Dried Grass Arrangement

A generous bunch of dried pampas grass, bunny tails, or wheat in a wide-mouthed vase creates a soft, textural arrangement that lasts indefinitely.

Dried grass arrangements require no water and no maintenance — place them once and leave them.

## 4. The Mixed Fresh and Dried Arrangement

Combine fresh dahlias or chrysanthemums with dried grasses, wheat, and eucalyptus in a single arrangement. The dried elements add texture and support the fresh stems.

Replace the fresh flowers as they fade and keep the dried elements in place.

## 5. The Foliage-Forward Arrangement

A vase of autumn branches with leaves still attached — beech, oak, or maple — creates a dramatic, seasonal arrangement that requires no flowers at all.

Cut branches in early autumn when the leaves are just beginning to turn for the best colour.

## 6. The Chrysanthemum Cluster

A tight cluster of chrysanthemums in a single colour — deep burgundy, rust, or warm yellow — in a low, wide vase creates a bold, graphic fall arrangement.

Chrysanthemums last two to three weeks in fresh water, making them one of the longest-lasting fall flowers.

## 7. The Wildflower and Seed Head Mix

A loose, informal arrangement of late-season wildflowers, seed heads, and dried grasses in a simple glass or ceramic vase suits a relaxed, natural fall aesthetic.

Forage seed heads and grasses from the garden or hedgerow for a free, authentic arrangement.

## 8. The Single Stem Vase Collection

A collection of small vases, each holding a single stem — one dahlia, one sunflower, one sprig of eucalyptus — creates a simple, modern fall display.

Group the vases together on a tray for a cohesive look.

## 9. The Amaranthus Cascade

Amaranthus — the long, drooping burgundy or green flower — creates a dramatic cascading arrangement in a tall vase. Combine it with dahlias and dried grasses for a full, abundant look.

## 10. The Dried Wreath Arrangement

A wreath made from dried grasses, wheat, and seed heads is a fall flower arrangement in a different form. Hang it on the wall, above the fireplace, or on the front door.

## Vessel Choices for Fall

The vessel matters as much as the flowers. For fall arrangements, choose:

- **Terracotta pots** — warm, earthy, and inexpensive
- **Dark ceramic vases** — deep green, chocolate brown, or matte black
- **Amber glass** — catches the light beautifully
- **Galvanised metal buckets** — suit a farmhouse or rustic aesthetic

Avoid clear glass for fall arrangements — it shows the stems and water, which can look untidy.

## Final Thoughts

Fall flower arrangements are most successful when they embrace the season's palette — warm, deep, and slightly muted — and mix textures as well as colours. Start with one or two fresh flowers and add dried grasses and foliage for texture. The result will look more interesting and last longer than a single-material arrangement.
