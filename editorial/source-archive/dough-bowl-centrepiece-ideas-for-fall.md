A fall dough bowl centrepiece is one of the most popular autumn decorating projects, and for good reason. The organic shape of the bowl adds warmth and texture that a flat tray cannot match, and the wide, shallow form is perfectly suited to the layered, abundant aesthetic of fall decorating.

This guide covers the exact method for building a fall dough bowl centrepiece that looks genuinely beautiful.

## Choosing the Bowl

A wooden dough bowl between 40cm and 60cm long is the most versatile size for a dining table centrepiece. A smaller bowl — 25–35cm — works well on a coffee table, sideboard, or mantelpiece.

The bowl does not need to be antique. New hand-carved bowls are widely available and the arrangement inside creates the visual impact.

## Step 1: The Base Layer

Fill the bowl with a generous base of dried autumn leaves, eucalyptus, and dried grasses. This is the most important step — a full, generous base is what makes the arrangement look abundant rather than sparse.

Let the greenery spill slightly over the edges of the bowl. This is what gives the arrangement its natural, organic quality.

## Step 2: The Candles

Add candles next, before any pumpkins or accent objects. Use an odd number — three or five — and vary the heights significantly.

A combination of a tall taper candle in a holder, a medium pillar candle, and a small votive creates the most interesting silhouette. Position the tallest candle slightly off-centre.

Use flameless LED candles if the arrangement will be left unattended or if the dried material is very close to the flame.

## Step 3: The Pumpkins and Gourds

Tuck small pumpkins and gourds among the greenery at different depths. Use an odd number and vary the sizes.

Choose pumpkins in a consistent colour palette — all cream, all rust, or a mix of cream and rust — rather than mixing every available colour.

## Step 4: Natural Accents

Pine cones, dried orange slices, cinnamon sticks, and acorns add texture and fragrance. Tuck them among the greenery and pumpkins, filling any gaps.

Dried orange slices can be made at home by slicing oranges thinly and drying them in the oven at 80°C for three to four hours.

## Step 5: Ribbon

A length of plaid, velvet, or wired ribbon woven through the arrangement adds colour and movement. Use a ribbon that picks up one of the colours in the pumpkins or candles.

Wired ribbon holds its shape better than unwired ribbon and is easier to arrange.

## Fall Colour Palette Options

- **Classic fall:** rust candles, orange and cream pumpkins, plaid ribbon
- **Neutral fall:** cream candles, white and cream pumpkins, natural linen ribbon
- **Farmhouse fall:** cream candles, mixed pumpkins, buffalo check ribbon
- **Moody fall:** deep burgundy candles, dark pumpkins, velvet ribbon in deep green

## Placement Options

- **Dining table:** keep the arrangement low — no taller than 25cm — so it does not block sightlines
- **Coffee table:** use a smaller bowl and a lower arrangement
- **Mantelpiece:** a longer, lower arrangement suits a mantelpiece well
- **Sideboard or console:** a more generous arrangement works here

## Caring for the Arrangement

Dried materials last the whole season without any maintenance. If you use fresh pumpkins, replace them when they begin to soften. Keep the arrangement away from direct heat sources to extend the life of any fresh elements.

## Final Thoughts

A fall dough bowl centrepiece is built in layers — base, candles, pumpkins, accents, ribbon — and each layer should be complete before the next one is added. Fill the bowl generously, let the arrangement spill over the edges, and use odd numbers throughout. The result will look warm, abundant, and genuinely autumnal.
