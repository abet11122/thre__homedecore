A small kitchen works best when daily cooking tasks have clear places and the counter remains usable for preparation. Before buying dividers or decanting containers, identify what you use every day, what can move to higher storage, and what is no longer needed.

For a quick first pass on a cabinet insert, compare your measurements with the product's outside dimensions in the [storage fit checker](/tools/storage-fit-checker/).

## Start with a one-week inventory

Note which pans, utensils, ingredients, and appliances you use during ordinary meals. Keep those close to where they are used. Group baking or party supplies separately if they are occasional. A rarely used appliance can occupy a cupboard; a heavy appliance used daily may need a safer, easier-to-reach location.

## Measure each cabinet opening

Record the *clear opening* width, depth to the back, usable height under hinges or plumbing, and distance needed for a drawer to pull out. An organizer that fits the cabinet box may still be too wide to pass through the opening. For under-sink storage, sketch the pipes and valves before choosing bins, and keep access to shutoffs clear.

| Area | Measure | Practical check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pan cabinet | Opening width and shelf height | Can each pan or lid come out without moving the stack? |
| Deep shelf | Depth and reachable distance | Would a pull-out bin make the back usable? |
| Counter | Uninterrupted preparation length | Can you set down a board and food safely? |
| Inside a cabinet door | Hinge and shelf clearance | Will the door still shut with an organizer attached? |

## Organize by task

Keep preparation tools near the main work surface, cooking tools near the hob, and daily dishes near the dishwasher or drying rack if possible. This reduces the number of trips across the kitchen. Avoid hanging towels or tools where they could contact a hot surface, and follow appliance clearance instructions.

## Compare three ways to free counter space

**Move it into a cabinet:** best for light, occasional tools if retrieval is easy. **Use a shelf riser or vertical divider:** useful when cabinet height is wasted, provided the upper item stays stable and reachable. **Keep it on the counter:** reasonable for a frequently used appliance if it leaves adequate preparation space and follows the maker's ventilation rules. The right choice depends on use frequency and actual fit, not on a blanket rule that counters must be empty.

## Illustrative cabinet example

Suppose a shelf has 60 cm of clear width but its door opening is 52 cm. A 55 cm pull-out tray cannot be installed through that opening even though it fits the shelf on paper. A pair of smaller bins may work better. This is only a planning example; measure the door, hinges, and any face frame in your kitchen.

## Reset the system after cooking

Give daily tools a return spot that is simple enough to use while tired. Keep broad categories for packets and utensils, and label only bins whose contents are otherwise hard to see. After two weeks, check what still lives on the counter. If it is used daily, consider making room for it; if it is not, move it or let it go.
