Christmas window decor is one of the highest-impact changes you can make to a home during the holidays. Windows are visible from the street, from the garden, and from inside every room — so a small amount of effort here goes a long way.

The ideas below work for renters and owners alike, and most require no tools.

## 1. Hang a Wreath in Each Window

A wreath centred in a window is a classic for good reason. Use a suction-cup hook on the glass or a tension rod across the frame to hang it without any fixings.

Choose a size that leaves a visible border of glass around the wreath — a wreath that fills the entire frame looks crowded.

## 2. Line the Sill With Battery Candles

A row of battery-powered candle lights on the windowsill creates a warm, unified glow that reads beautifully from outside. Use candles of the same height for a clean line.

Warm-white flames around 2700K look more realistic than cool-white options.

## 3. Drape a Simple Garland Across the Top

A garland draped along the top of the window frame adds greenery without blocking light. Pin it at the corners and let it bow gently in the middle.

Eucalyptus, pine, and cedar all hold their shape well and smell good while they last.

## 4. Add Frosted Window Film

Frosted or snowflake-patterned window film is a renter-friendly option that adds a wintry look without blocking light. It peels off cleanly at the end of the season.

Apply it to the lower half of the window to maintain the view while adding texture.

## 5. Use a Tension Rod and Hanging Ornaments

A tension rod fitted inside the window frame lets you hang ornaments at different heights using ribbon or twine. This works especially well in a kitchen or bathroom window.

Choose ornaments in two or three colours rather than mixing every colour you own.

## 6. Frame the Window With Fairy Lights

Fairy lights taped or clipped around the inside of the window frame create a glowing border. Use warm-white lights and keep the cable tidy by running it along the edge of the frame.

This works best in a room that is used in the evenings, where the effect is most visible.

## 7. Place a Small Tree on the Sill

A small tabletop tree on a wide windowsill is visible from outside and adds a festive focal point inside. Keep it simple — a few lights and a handful of ornaments is enough.

Make sure the tree does not block ventilation or sit against a heat source.

## 8. Use Ribbon to Tie Back Curtains

Swapping your usual curtain ties for a length of velvet or satin ribbon in a seasonal colour is a small change that makes a noticeable difference.

Red, gold, and deep green all work well against neutral curtain fabric.

## 9. Create a Window Vignette on the Sill

A small grouping of objects on the sill — a candle, a small ornament, a sprig of greenery — creates a styled moment that is visible from outside.

Keep the grouping to one side of the sill so it does not block light from the centre of the window.

## 10. Add Snowflake Decals

Removable snowflake decals are an inexpensive option that works well in children's rooms or any window where you want a playful, wintry look.

Apply them to clean, dry glass and they will peel off without residue at the end of the season.

## Final Thoughts

The best Christmas window decor is simple, intentional, and visible from both sides of the glass. Start with one or two windows — the front-facing ones that are most visible from the street — and build from there. A consistent approach across all windows looks more considered than a different treatment in every room.
